# I Quit Law at 40. The Real Meaning of CEO Revealed During My 8-Year Journey.

5am in a Salt Lake parking garage. An 85 year old partner ahead of me. That walk ended my law firm career and started 8 years of trying to become a CEO.

It was 5:00 am in Salt Lake City, nine years ago, right after I turned 40. I was walking through the parking garage toward the elevator, cold concrete echoing under my feet, heading to my law office.

Five feet in front of me walked my senior law partner. He was 85 years old. Wearing the exact same suit from yesterday with his battered leather briefcase. His shoes scuffed at the toes, and his shoulders slumped under a weight he had been carrying for decades. He kept his eyes locked on the floor with every slow step.

I stared at the back of his coat and felt a cold wave of panic hit my chest.

*Is this me in 40 years?*
*Giving everything to my clients for their legacy, not mine?*

I did not take another step. I turned back to my car and drove straight home. I walked into the house, looked at my wife Jaime and said "Today we transition to building our real estate empire." She held my hand and approved "Let's do it."

That was the day the old path died.

If you operate real estate or co-invest in deals, you know that feeling if you are building your legacy or someone else's.

Today, I am not the only one rethinking the title CEO. Forbes ran a piece calling it Chief Evangelist of the Organization. Substack writers call for a Chief Discerner in the age of AI, whose remaining job is judgment while machines run execution. The old CEO definition is stale during this AI revolution.

Meanwhile big money still runs joint ventures with paperwork to match. On September 17, Ares Management and PSP Investments formed a JV to invest up to $2.4 billion in US logistics real estate with a 5.2 million square foot seed portfolio across California, Texas and New Jersey. Small operators run the same JV label with none of that discipline. That gap is where compliance risk lives.

I have my own version of CEO, built during eight years of joint ventures, risking my own money and reputation.

The term CEO defined by my guiding principles: **Create. Execute. Optimize.**

## Principle 1: Create

You live your life in one of two states: a suffering state or a beautiful state. You cannot inhabit both at the same time.

Suffering looks like chasing docs across email, texts, and shared drives while money moves without approval. Beautiful looks like one transparent dashboard for company details, bank accounts linked, docs secure, and audited ledgers.

When you sit stuck in a suffering state, trapped by routine or anxiety, the only way back to a beautiful state is creativity. You step back into your identity as a creator. The highest form of power on this planet.

When I made that decision in the parking garage, I launched an eight year run of pure creation. I reached out to my CEO clients and asked them to mentor me. I read hundreds of business and personal development books. I hired a coach.

Then I asked Richard Giles, one of my greatest mentors, to bring me on as a co-venture partner. My first joint venture.

Rich was a serial entrepreneur and inventor with multiple patents, using cutting edge technology to disrupt construction. He was over 60, but he looked and acted 35, with a broad and bright smile. He lived in Park City, Utah with his beautiful wife, Bratis Peralta.

We scheduled a meeting for 9:00 am on a freezing, snowy Park City morning. I arrived at my office an hour early. My hands ice cold from nerves. I got down on my knees and prayed. I asked God for courage and to take away fears of failure and rejection.

At 9:00 am, Rich walked in with his dog SingiBear. After some small talk, I took my shot. "Rich, can I join you as a business partner, rather than working as your lawyer?"

Rich went silent. "Give me the evening to think it over. Same time. Same place. Tomorrow."

The very next day at 9:00 am sharp, Rich walked into my Park City office. No hello. Just a big smile, and he handed me a large yellow envelope. Inside was a printed stock certificate with my name on it. Rich shook my hand and said "Let's go partner." I still have this stock certificate today to remind me to take a risk on others willing to co-create your empire.

The second he walked out the door, I pounded my chest with a triple fist pump, flexed like Hulk Hogan, and celebrated over lunch with a large Mountain Dew. That single creative moment launched me into dozens of active joint venture partnerships over eight years.

Utah rewards that kind of creation right now if you bring structure. Utah median listing price sat at $579,000 in August 2026 with inventory at a 12 year high. More choice means operators who run clean books close while handshake deals stall.

Pick one operator you admire. Ask to build one deal together this quarter.

## Principle 2: Execute

As I worked in these joint ventures, I had to rethink the second principle: Execute.

Most people treat execution like endless task grinding. I did for the first several years.

Execution is the work of removing resistance. Resistance is opposition to your natural curiosity and ultimate power state. Resistance creates static, friction, and misalignment. Picture an arrow drawn back on a bowstring. The arrow flies after that resistance is released.

Your limitations are the resistance holding you back.

A belief is a decision you made, at some point, about yourself or circumstance: your capacity, your wealth, your health, your relationships. Once you made that decision, your brain went hunting for evidence to prove you were right.

Similarly, you hold the power to make a new belief. A new decision. When you make a new decision, you change your thoughts, your emotions, and your subconscious behavior.

Take a common limiting belief: "More money equals more problems."

Decide the opposite. Right now.

"More Money equals More Solutions."
"Unlimited Money equals Unlimited Solutions."

Your new decision reshapes your perspective. Decision drives belief. Belief drives thought. Thought drives emotion. Emotion drives behavior. Behavior yields the result that confirms your new reality. (This is mindset, not a return promise.)

Execution also means you stop guessing on securities rules. Under SEC Rule 506(b) you cannot use general solicitation, you may include up to 35 non accredited purchasers who must be sophisticated, and you must file Form D within 15 days after first sale. Under Rule 506(c) you may advertise broadly only if every purchaser is accredited and you take reasonable steps to verify that status with documents like W-2s, tax returns, or brokerage statements. Instagram ads plus 506(b) breaks the exemption. Sources: SEC Rule 506 overview and Rule 506(c) verification guide.

Execute today by eliminating resistance through new decisions, then stay fiercely loyal to them.

## Principle 3: Optimize

Two years ago, I connected with Bob Beaudine, who Sports Illustrated calls "the most influential man in sports you've never heard of." We became fast friends.

In one of our recent meetups, Bob handed me a signed copy of his book [*2 Chairs*](https://www.bobbeaudine.com/). Reading it clarified a principle I felt my whole life without language for: the power of co-creation.

Optimization is not about working yourself to exhaustion trying to control every variable. It is about inviting God into your plan every morning and co-creating your day with Him. You do your part. You surrender the rest to Him to heal, repair, create, and empower.

One of my other mentors, [Allyson Chavez](https://allysonchavez.com/), recently taught me a powerful exercise I now use daily.

"I can't. He can. I let Him."

Surrendering control is the doorway to optimization. As long as you believe you can repair everything on your own, you refuse to truly surrender. You cannot optimize since many actions were never meant for you alone.

The moment you realize every major success in your life was and is co-created with a higher power, you stop carrying the stress alone. You allow God to become your joint venture partner.

Surrender the outcome. Operate the rules daily. Then you will optimize.

The market now favors operators who do this boring work. The CRE Report found tertiary markets passed 50 percent of transactions across office, retail, apartments and industrial in the year through June 2026, up from about 34 percent for primary markets. Smaller markets mean thinner teams and more partner capital. Clean ledgers travel. Dark books do not.

## Build Your CEO Playbook

Look at your current trajectory. Are you walking toward an elevator carrying an old briefcase, eyes locked on the floor? Are you living in a suffering state? Are you repeating limiting beliefs? Are you going at it alone?

You do not have to stay on that track. Shift your definition of what it means to lead your fullest life:

**Create**: Step out of suffering by choosing to build something new. I asked Rich to become his partner. Ask for what you want, even when your hands are cold with fear.

**Execute**: Strip away the resistance. Allow emerging technologies to replace chaos. Replace outdated limiting decisions with new ones that serve who you want to become.

**Optimize**: Stop trying to carry the world solo. My morning surrender and evening tech audit. Practice daily co-creation and let a higher power handle the heavy lifting.
