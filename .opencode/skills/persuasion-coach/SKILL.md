## 1. The 5 types (canon, do not change)

These are Jared definitions. Use them verbatim for diagnosis and drafting.

### Director: buys freedom
Risk taker, pioneer, independent thinker. Wants independence and control. Drawn to unique and ground breaking solutions. Seeks experiences that push boundaries.
Tells: skims, decides fast, asks about options and control, hates restrictions and long setup.
Sell: give options, not restrictions. Show how the product provides freedom, makes them a trailblazer, or lets them run life on their terms.
Proof they trust: short options list, control kept, exit clear, numbers up front.

### Relator: buys emotion, relationship, community
Cares about people. Values products that strengthen connections. Attracted to items that help inspire others. Prefers solutions that enhance caregiving and support.
Tells: asks about who else is in, shares personal context, wants alignment before numbers, forwards to a partner.
Sell: show how this improves relationships, enhances influence, or lets them serve and support loved ones.
Proof they trust: names, stories, partner alignment, peer voices.

### Intellectual: buys proof
Logic driven, analytical, always seeking knowledge and validation. Seeks products that expand knowledge. Values tools that streamline tasks. Drawn to wellness and environmentally connected products.
Tells: asks for mechanism, sources, edge cases, reads fine print, slow to decide, fears buyer remorse.
Sell: speak with logic and depth. Provide researched insight, emphasize organization and efficiency, connect to knowledge, health, or high standards.
Proof they trust: mechanism explained, sources linked, comparisons, audit trail shown.

### Validator: buys identity
Status driven, recognition seeking, prestige oriented. Prefers brands that enhance prestige. Values proven quality and credibility. Drawn to exclusivity and social validation.
Tells: asks who else uses this, checks credentials, notices craft and polish, wants to be early and right.
Sell: give respect and recognition. Show the product elevates status, builds trust, and reinforces personal value.
Proof they trust: named operators, media, specific results, founding member status.

### Executive: buys success
Goal oriented, competitive, results driven. Seeks tools for success and competition. Prefers solutions that enhance authority. Drawn to products that ensure results and security.
Tells: asks about ROI, timeline, risk controls, what winning looks like in 90 days.
Sell: focus on results. Show how this helps them win, stay in control, and hit measurable results with security and efficiency.
Proof they trust: waterfall math, timeline, controls, downside protection.

### Detector (use on any comment, DM, or reply)
- Pace fast plus asks price or options: Director or Executive. Ask: is the question about freedom (Director) or winning (Executive)?
- Shares story or asks about people: Relator.
- Asks how it works, what source, what if: Intellectual.
- Asks who uses it, what rank, what credential: Validator.
- Asks what return, what timeline, what guarantee: Executive.
If mixed signals, pick primary plus secondary. Example: Director plus Intellectual. Lead with options, back with proof.

## 2. The 8 drives mapped to the 5 types

Backbone is Octalysis 8 Core Drives. Pick the top 2 drives per type. Name them in your plan line before drafting.

1. Epic Meaning and Calling: be part of something larger. Best for Relator, Validator.
2. Development and Accomplishment: progress, mastery, milestones. Best for Intellectual, Executive.
3. Empowerment of Creativity and Feedback: choices, feedback, control of method. Best for Director, Intellectual.
4. Ownership and Possession: own, improve, protect what is mine. Best for Director, Validator, Executive.
5. Social Influence and Relatedness: belonging, mentorship, peer proof. Best for Relator, Validator.
6. Scarcity and Impatience: want what is limited. Best for Executive, Validator, Director. Use only real scarcity.
7. Unpredictability and Curiosity: want to know what happens next. Best for Director, Intellectual. Use for hooks.
8. Loss and Avoidance: avoid loss, protect downside. Best for Executive, Intellectual, Relator. Strongest for compliance topics.

Map table:
- Director: lead 3 plus 4, support 7. Example angles: run your own structure, keep control of bank and votes, see every dollar.
- Relator: lead 1 plus 5, support 8. Example angles: protect partners, keep trust intact, avoid the fight that breaks relationships.
- Intellectual: lead 2 plus 3, support 8. Example angles: five checks, audit log, mechanism shown step by step.
- Validator: lead 5 plus 4, support 1. Example angles: founding 100, named operators, craft matches the asset.
- Executive: lead 2 plus 8, support 6. Example angles: close faster, control risk, lock the structure before capital moves.

White hat versus black hat balance: lead with white hat (1, 2, 3) for trust. Add one black hat (6, 7, 8) for motion. Never stack two black hat lines in a short post. One urgency line per Note or LinkedIn post max.

## 3. Language bank (copy patterns, adapt specifics)

Do not invent numbers, names, or deal terms. Use placeholders like [deal city], [amount], [days] if facts are missing, then ask Jared for the fact.

Director verbs: choose, keep, control, run, set, skip, approve, exit.
- Openers: "You have three ways to run this." "Keep signing authority." "Pick the structure, then fund."
- Proof frames: "Options: A, B, C. You pick." "You approve debt before it moves." "Exit defined on page [X]."
- Close: "Pick your path and start this week."

Relator verbs: protect, support, align, serve, keep trust, bring, share.
- Openers: "Your partners trusted you with [amount]." "Bring your partner into alignment before the lot purchase." "This keeps Thanksgiving normal."
- Proof frames: "[Name] in [city] said [verbatim quote]." "Three partners see one screen." "No thread hunt."
- Close: "Forward this to your partner."

Intellectual verbs: verify, show, log, map, test, compare, document.
- Openers: "Here is the mechanism." "Five checks before capital moves." "Compare 506(b) versus 506(c) on one page."
- Proof frames: "Source: [link]." "Step 1, 2, 3 with receipts." "Audit log shows date, amount, approver."
- Close: "Read the checklist, then decide."

Validator verbs: recognized, proven, founding, selected, featured, trusted.
- Openers: "Founding 100 operators." "Built for $2M to $12M specs." "Reviewed by a 20 year attorney."
- Proof frames: "Operator in [city] closed [result] in [days]." "As seen in [outlet]." "Flat fee stated upfront."
- Close: "Claim a founding seat."

Executive verbs: win, close, hit, lock, secure, control, measure.
- Openers: "Close in [days] with clean books." "One screen: company, bank, docs, ledger." "Stop the leak that costs [amount]."
- Proof frames: "Waterfall: [terms]." "Debt cap: [terms]." "Distributions resume when [trigger]."
- Close: "Lock your next deal this month."

Banned moves: fake scarcity, fake countdowns, vague testimonials ("great agent"), invented stats, passive voice where action matters, scene setting throat clearing ("Today I want to talk about").

## 4. Cialdini stacker (2 to 3 per piece, no more)

- Reciprocity: give one usable thing free. Market data, checklist, three questions to ask a builder, fee disclosure template.
- Commitment and Consistency: ask for one micro yes. Comment YES, save post, reply MAP, forward to partner.
- Social Proof: specific and situated. Name plus city plus amount plus days. "Sarah M. in Promontory closed $8.7M spec terms in 21 days" beats "highly recommend."
- Authority: show, do not claim. 20 years, $2B transactions, 1,000 plus foreclosures and workouts, SEC rules cited with links.
- Liking: one human line. Socks at Showcase, tears in first draft, in the build with operators.
- Scarcity: real only. 100 operators cap, 10 at a time for 1 on 1 work, monthly onboarding window. If verified on screenshot, it must hold true.
- Unity: shared identity. Active operators, not passive check writers. Builders who run clean books.

Stack recipes:
- Short post: 1 value (Reciprocity) plus 1 proof (Social Proof or Authority) plus 1 motion (real Scarcity or Loss frame).
- Article: Reciprocity early, Authority plus Social Proof mid, Commitment mid (three questions walk), Unity plus Scarcity at CTA.
Ethics guardrail: if a claim would fail a screenshot test by a journalist, cut it. No manufactured deadlines. No "only 2 left" for unlimited supply. Projections labeled as projections with what must go right.

## 5. Real estate pain library (use one per post)

Pick one. Lead with hook, back with fact.

1. Passive equals security. If money partners only write checks and wait, the interest looks like a security. Label JV does not fix it. Risk: rescission plus interest even if the house would have sold well.
2. Solicitation trap. 506(b) bans general solicitation. 506(c) allows it but requires verified accredited status for every buyer. Instagram ads plus 506(b) breaks the exemption.
3. Missing Form D and state notices. Late or missing filings show on EDGAR and surface in lender diligence and the next raise.
4. No PPM means no proof. Without a dated signed disclosure doc, you argue from emails and calls when an investor claims omission.
5. Fee silence. Undisclosed acquisition, management, construction, disposition, or affiliate fees read as conflicts. List who gets paid, how much, on what base.
6. Commingled funds. Investor cash in operator account, customer deposits routed around the partnership account. Loss of traceability is itself a red flag.
7. No fundamental decisions list. Debt, refi, affiliate contracts, asset sale, overruns, tax allocations need prior approval. Vague authority stalls builds.
8. Capital call shock. Extra cash needed mid build. Dilution or forfeiture clauses surprise partners. State rules upfront.
9. Dark bank feed. Five questions test: partnership account, signed JV Terms, loan draw approval, related party fee vote, audit log. Silence equals risk.
10. Exit drag. ROFR notices, valuation fights, forced sales, tax hits. Define buy sell mechanics and valuation tie breaker early.

Each pain maps to JV OS five checks: company created, bank connected, JV agreement signed, LLC operating agreement signed, first contribution logged with receipt.

## 7. Rotation rule (default, approved)

Weekly rotation for daily posts. One type per day so the list hears itself over time.
- Monday Director (freedom, options)
- Tuesday Relator (trust, partners)
- Wednesday Intellectual (mechanism, proof)
- Thursday Validator (status, founding)
- Friday Executive (win, controls)

Long article rule: stack all 5 in one piece via proof paragraph order: Director options line, Relator story line, Intellectual mechanism lines, Validator credential line, Executive results line. Then single CTA.

Then draft. No plan line in final published copy.

## 8. Workflow

1. Detect type from prompt or pick rotation day. If user says "write for [type]", use that type.
2. Pick one pain from section 5. Pick top 2 drives from section 2.
3. Pick stack from section 4 (max 3).
4. Draft in template from section 6. Use language bank from section 3.
5. Apply humanize-writing check: open on subject, vary length, cut runway, add one specific detail, take a position, plain verbs.
6. Apply offer-playbook check for CTA posts: specific outcome, named deliverable (JV OS five checks), mechanism named (active JV), risk reversal (straight fit answer), exclusion (no passive seats).
7. Verify: one CTA per short post, real numbers only, UTM correct, no mailto, no em dash character, no antithesis phrasing.
8. Deliver: final copy plus 2 line note (type plus drives used, pain used). Keep note outside copy block.
